# Open research

What the record has to settle, with what is known so far. Sightings by Alex DeJarnatt on
Newspapers.com, 2026-10-31, unless noted. The specific pages to find are in `wanted.tsv` and on
the status page.

## 1. When did Bell start?

The first E&P Syndicate Directory (1924-10-25) lists Bell's crossword as twice a week, and the
catalog started it there. Bell was selling it at least eight months earlier: the Indianapolis
Star of Sunday 1924-02-03 has a Bell copyright, which confirms Crossword Craze's February 1924.
That is the earliest Bell crossword we know of, not necessarily the first.

## 2. Is there one Bell crossword or two?

A Bell ad in the same October 1924 E&P issue offers "Two Crackerjack Puzzle Services", and a
Bell ad in the July 1925 issue names "Criss-X-Word Puzzles" (catalog census).

- **Unnumbered.** Indianapolis 1924-02-03 has no number. Burlington's crop of 1924-09-18 shows
  none: "Cross Word Puzzle", then a short "Introduction" saying where beginners may start, then
  "(Copyright 1924 by the Bell Syndicate, Inc.)", then the grid, so the line is the puzzle's.
- **Numbered.** Yonkers Statesman, "Puzzle No. 11" on Saturday 1924-09-27 and "Puzzle No. 12" on
  Saturday 10-04, the second with "How to Solve the Cross Word Puzzle", "Solution of Last Week's
  Puzzle" and "(Copyright, 1924 by The Bell Syndicate, Inc.)".

Yonkers announced on Friday 09-26 that its crossword would run every Saturday from tomorrow, so
the series opens there at No. 11. That suggests the number is Bell's, not Yonkers's, but Yonkers
is the only numbered paper so far, so it is not proved. Weekly, No. 1 falls on 1924-07-19, after
Burlington's start and well after Indianapolis's, so one weekly count from February does not
reach it: Burlington's 09-18 puzzle is its 28th if it missed no week. A second service, or a
count begun that July, would explain it. Best test: whether Burlington's 09-25 or 10-02 puzzle
is Yonkers's No. 11 or No. 12, and any other paper's Bell puzzle with a number.

## 3. How did papers take the feature?

Weekly, each on its own day: Indianapolis on Sunday, Burlington on Thursday, Yonkers on
Saturday. Burlington's 1924-03-13 page says the solution will be in next Thursday's Free Press
and prints no earlier solution, so it may be the paper's first, as Crossword Craze has it. It is
the same puzzle as Indianapolis's of Sunday 03-16. That is Indianapolis's 7th if it missed no
week from 02-03, so Burlington joined a series already running, and a paper's first Bell puzzle
says nothing about Bell's first. Are copies always within a few days, or does the lag grow?

## 4. When did Bell go daily?

E&P has twice a week in October 1924 and daily and Sunday by July 1925. Yonkers went daily the
week of 1924-10-13: Saturday 10-11 announces a Monday puzzle, and puzzles follow on 10-13, 10-14
(with "answer to yesterday's puzzle") and 10-15, with no number that week. Only the Saturdays
seen carry the Bell line, so those weekday puzzles may not be Bell's. The LA Times's Bell daily
starts 1924-11-27 (the page printed "The Ball Syndicate"). If the Yonkers weekdays are Bell's,
the daily is six weeks older than the catalog has it.

## 5. Who made the puzzles?

No constructor seen so far. E&P's 1924 listing says only "Various"; compare McClure's by Richard
H. Tingley.

## After July 1925

Outside this record's scope, so not in `printings.tsv`; kept here for when the later years open.

- **Seattle Post-Intelligencer**, Friday 1927-06-03: a Bell Syndicate crossword with "yesterday's
  solution", so the paper was running it on consecutive days, not weekly. No date yet for when
  it started.

## Finding them

Text search works for "Bell Syndicate" near "cross word", but OCR varies and printers slipped:
the LA Times has both "The Ball Syndicate" and a copyright "1921". Burlington's intro for
beginners and Yonkers's "How to Solve the Cross Word Puzzle" are more words to try, though the
second is generic. A Bell line elsewhere on the page proves nothing: many pages run a Bell story
or column, and the Boston Globe's pages with a Bell line in newyorkworld's `printings.tsv` are
all of that kind.

## Why the Wheeler link is worth a look

John Neville Wheeler, Bell's founder, was the first editor of Liberty magazine, 1924 to early
1926, while still running Bell (Wikipedia, not yet checked against a primary source). Liberty
ran weekly crosswords by 1929 (see newyorkworld's RESEARCH.md). Whether its puzzles have any
Bell connection is an open question.
